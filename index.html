01  //  about

I build systems that behave
under real load.

I'm a backend engineer who thinks in terms of the life of a request: what it touches, where it can fail, and how it keeps moving when a dependency drops out. Most of my work lives in Go — building APIs, worker pools, streams, and the glue that keeps them honest.

I care about the parts of a system that don't show up on a healthy day: bounds on retries, poisoning records that never get a graceful path out, a context that gets cancelled a layer too early, and the difference between a queue that's full and a queue that's dead.

My bias is simple systems that scale predictably, are observable by default, and fail gracefully when they have to.

06  //  principles

What I hold
systems to.

  • /01

    Design for failure.

    Every component is a dependency that can go down. Plan the outage, not the uptime.

  • /02

    Make concurrency observable.

    A goroutine you can't see is a goroutine you can't debug. Instrument queues, pools, and cancellations.

  • /03

    Prefer simple systems that scale predictably.

    The architecture you can reason about is the one you can operate at 3am.

  • /04

    Measure before optimizing.

    Profile first, tune second. The hot path is rarely the one you guessed.

  • /05

    Retries need boundaries.

    Unbounded retries are a distributed infinite loop. Add jitter, backoff, and a hard cap.

  • /06

    A healthy system fails gracefully.

    Shed load, isolate poison records, and let the rest of the traffic keep moving.
